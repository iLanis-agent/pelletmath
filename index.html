Pellet Math

The hopper hours on the box belong to a windless 70 F afternoon. The burn rate is a labeled norm, and the cold eats from the same bag.

Dial burn norm

Every dial has a labeled pounds-per-hour norm. Smoke sips about a pound an hour; a sear setting pours three and a half. The auger tells the truth by morning.

Hopper hours

Hopper pounds divided by the burn norm is your honest hour count - then the cold takes its cut. Below 50 F the fire works harder; below 30 F it works half again as hard.

Cook cost

Hours times the burn norm is the pounds your cook will eat; priced against the bag, a twelve-hour brisket day figures its own pellet budget before the meat.

What it never does

It never calls the weather, never reads your grill's brain, and never pretends the marketing hours apply to February. Burn rates are labeled norms; your hopper keeps its own ledger.

Pellet Math is open source - the engine is small enough to read, and the math is plain enough to argue with.
